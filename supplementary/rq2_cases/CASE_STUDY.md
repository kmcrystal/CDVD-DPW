# RQ2 qualitative case analysis

We analyze four target-test functions to illustrate when cross-domain structural evidence supports correct detection and when incomplete or misleading dependence context causes errors. Two cases are drawn from LibPNG→LibTIFF, while the false positive and false negative cover both directions of the difficult Pidgin--Asterisk setting.

No equivalent destination-capacity guard dominates these writes in the extracted dependence context. | **LibPNG `png_handle_sPLT` (`cve-2004-0599-1.c`): a chunk length controls allocation, pointer advancement, loop bounds, and structure-array writes, forming a similar length→address/copy→index dependence chain.** | Transfer succeeds through a structural relation rather than an API-name match: both functions connect externally controlled lengths with memory movement and index progression without an equivalent capacity constraint before the relevant writes. |

## Audited case statistics

- `t2p_process_jpeg_strip` belongs to the LibTIFF U95 target-test partition and has 35 unique model-input nodes: 30 AU, 3 AE, and 2 PU.
- `checkAdd64` belongs to the LibTIFF U95 target-test partition and has 2 unique model-input nodes: 1 AE and 1 PU.
- `gen_prios` belongs to the Pidgin→Asterisk U95 target-test partition. It has 402 metric-eligible unique nodes derived from 425 slice records; PU and string-related function-call candidates dominate.
- `mxit_parse_packet` belongs to the Asterisk→Pidgin U95 target-test partition. It has 19 metric-eligible unique nodes derived from 33 slice records: 10 PU, 6 AU, 2 AE, and 1 `memset` call node.

Together, the cases show that cross-domain transfer is strongest when source and target share a comparable dependence structure, and degrades when local syntactic similarity masks project-specific protection constraints or when the vulnerability depends on long-range state and boundary interactions.
